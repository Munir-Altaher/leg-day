## Design assumptions (verify on build day)

- **Motor output face.** The Ø27 / Ø15-boss face is assumed to be the rotor and the 1 mm Ø37 step to be on that face, so the thigh and adapter touch only the rotor. Turn each motor by hand before printing. If it's wrong, the motor faces flip and the stack changes.
- **Motor boss height.** Assumed 1.0 mm proud (`MOTOR_BOSS_H`); pockets are 1.5 mm deep. Measure it.
- **Cable exit.** Lead positions are unknown, so every motor's back plate has a Ø20 centre hole. Check the real exit and add a notch if needed.
- **Rotor inertia.** The catalog value of 540 g·cm² may be rotor-side or output-side. The reflected-inertia numbers assume rotor-side × gear². Verify before quoting it in the pitch.
- **No CubeMars STEP used.** The motors are simplified bodies (Ø53 × 37 plus the Ø37 × 1 step). Download the official STEP and swap it into the assembly to check details.
- **PLA bearing seats** use +0.10 mm on Ø16. Print the coupon first and update `FIT_BEARING`.
- **Knee axle torque path.** Torque goes M5 bolt head (hex pocket in the shank) → bolt → M5 nylock in a hex pocket in the pulley. Use medium threadlocker on the nut. At the 0.76 Nm firmware limit the knee sees 2.3 Nm, well under the breakaway torque of a locked M5.
- **Hip-pulley shaft** is an M5 × 16 (head in the adapter's hex pocket); it ends 0.6 mm short of the hip motor's face, inside the thigh's through-hole. The first hand-in said M5 × 25, which would have reached about 4 mm into the motor. File a flat for the kit pulley's grub screws.
- **The idler sits inside the belt loop** and pushes the front span outward (slot travel 9 mm). Wrap on the 20T pulley stays ≈ 180°. An outside idler would add wrap, but the channel has no room for one.
- **Mass factor.** Printed links are estimated at 0.75 × solid volume (4 perimeters + 40 % gyroid on 3–10 mm walls). Weigh the printed parts.
- **Ab/ad hard stops are not modelled.** Mechanically the stage is free to ±98°, so ±45° is enforced in firmware and by the cable service loop. Add printed stop lugs on the spine if the judges want physical stops.
- **Knee hard stops are not modelled either.** The knee is mechanically free from −30° to 152° (with the snowshoe), so the 15–145° limits are firmware-only. The snowshoe's ankle has physical stops (two printed lips, 11° and 52°). The hip stop is physical: the C-frame column, at ±107°.

## Things that fought back (each took > 15 min, so the simpler option was used)

- The SolidWorks API would not create reference planes at negative offsets (SolidWorks hung). Every part is therefore modelled in the body frame shifted by +300 mm on X, Y and Z (`swlib.SHIFT`). **Each part's origin sits at (−300, −300, −300) mm in the assembly.** The assemblies place every part in its true home pose, with the assembly origin at the hip-pitch / ab-ad axis intersection.
- Mates are generated by `cad/build_assembly.py` (named axes + plane coincidences + limit angle mates) and checked by `cad/verify_mates.py`. Angle limits in CAD are unsigned (see `docs/ASSEMBLY.md`).
- **Skeleton target partly met.** The shank is 34 % lighter (bent channel section + 3 rectangle cutouts in the web), the thigh 8 % (3 rectangle cutouts per wall, limited to the belt-free strip between the belt spans) (its walls are mostly keep-solid around the belt). Thigh + shank together is 15 % lighter, against the 30 % target. Link inertia about the hip is 28 % lower. See `analysis/out/skeleton.md`.
